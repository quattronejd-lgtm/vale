## CUT 3 — LinkedIn re-cut
*Professional pain first; the supply/demand thesis is the spine. Pairs with the 10-slide Field Notes carousel.*

Every marketing leader I know is carrying the same quiet math problem: consistent, on-brand social output costs either a hire, an agency retainer, or your evenings.

Yesterday I solved it a fourth way. Using Claude Code, I built an employee that takes source content from a WordPress site, transforms it into a designed, on-brand card, and publishes it to Instagram — four times a day, unattended. It was built in one day. It lives in GitHub, so it can be pointed at any brand. Its payroll is approximately zero.

Here's why that matters more than the build itself.

Content distribution is a supply-and-demand market. To get what you want from a platform — reach and engagement — you have to give the platform what it wants: supply. Instagram cannot keep users on-platform by bottlenecking content creation on its own taste and judgment. It has to incentivize creators to make content, and the currency it pays in is distribution. Most brands lose that exchange not on quality — on consistency. The feed is hungry every day; the team isn't.

An agent doesn't have that problem. Harold never misses a payday.

And the day of building looked nothing like the AI hype reel. I didn't write code or open a design tool. I made decisions: locked the visual design sixteen minutes in, approved dry runs before anything touched a live feed, gave art-director notes six pixels at a time. Thirteen hours after the first commit it published to the Good News Network's Instagram — @goodnewsnetwork, 48,000 followers. You can go look at the work right now. It posted twelve times on day one; I kept the six that earned their place. Taste didn't get automated. Taste got promoted — it's now the whole job.

What's next is the part I'm most excited about: I'm turning it on myself. I've already written a book. An agent will serialize it into Substack articles, and Harold will transcreate each one into Instagram and Facebook posts. One source, every platform, on schedule — while I coach.

The human is the hero. AI is the companion. That's the operating model, and it's how I build now: on my terms.
