02 · The lockup

One mark. Two grounds.

The lockup — designed by Shaana — is the brand's signature and supersedes any earlier mark or lockup guidance. Three parts, always together in primary use: the SaVvy wordmark, the linked-rule divider, and the tracked LAB SOLUTIONS sublabel. The chain link is the collective, drawn as a mark: labs linked together buy like the biggest.

Clear space & minimum size

Keep a clear zone of one cap-height of the S (call it 1S) on all four sides — nothing enters it: no copy, no rules, no photography focal points. Minimum width: 140 px digital / 32 mm print for the full lockup. Below that, drop the divider and sublabel and use the wordmark alone.

Do

  • Use the full three-part lockup in primary placements.
  • Champagne on onyx; deep gold on cream; ink when one-color.
  • Scale the whole lockup as one unit — proportions are fixed.
  • Wordmark alone below minimum size, or as the compact mark (headers, favicons, social avatars).

Don't

  • Recolor, add gradients, or apply rose gold / two-tone gold.
  • Separate or rearrange the divider and sublabel.
  • Set champagne directly on white or cream.
  • Place over photography without an onyx scrim behind it.
  • Stretch, outline, shadow, or re-set in another typeface.

Master assets: Shaana's original PNG is the reference of record; production vector (SVG/EPS) and the OC Bartok font files are being collected into savvy/assets/. The lockup rendered throughout this book is a live vector recreation — swap in the master files when they land.

03 · Color

Onyx and champagne. No brown.

Neutral onyx darks with a single warm accent — champagne. Champagne is the only warm element on the page. Dark surfaces stay true neutral onyx (never warm ink-black) so black and champagne read crisp and never drift toward brown. On light, use cream grounds with ink text and deep gold for gold lettering.

04 · Typography

The brand serif leads. Inter handles the rest.

Display / headlines — OC Bartok Light (the client's serif; until the font file is in hand this book falls back to Cormorant 300, then Georgia). It sets the wordmark and every headline, so the logo and the copy speak the same voice. Tight leading, light weight, large sizes.

Body / UI — Inter (400/500/600). Body at 16px / 1.7. Also used for tracked eyebrows and labels: uppercase, 0.3–0.5em letter-spacing, champagne.

The sublabel — "LAB SOLUTIONS" in the lockup — is set in the lockup's own tracked light sans and lives only there: never headlines, never body. (Supersedes v1: the Cinzel reservation is retired; the final lockup's sublabel face replaces it.)

06 · Avoidances

Never.

  • No brown grounds.
  • No gold gradients on type or marks; never rose gold.
  • No lockup sublabel face in body copy.
  • No more than one champagne accent competing in a single view.
  • No emoji or exclamation-heavy copy.
  • Never say "buying group" — it is a collective.
  • Never use the "room / take your seat" metaphor.

08 · Imagery

Craft, shot like luxury.

Photography treats the everyday craft of the lab — hands at the bench, restorations, materials, the quiet precision of the work — with the lighting and restraint of a luxury brand. Warm neutral light, deep shadow, shallow focus. Grade toward onyx and champagne; never candy-colored, never cheesy clinical stock.

Placeholder duotone tiles — swap for graded photography. Duotone (onyx→champagne) is for graphic moments, hero panels only, sparingly. Text over image always sits on an onyx scrim (bottom gradient to ~60% black) so champagne and white stay legible.

Do

  • Warm-neutral editorial lighting.
  • Real work, real hands, real materials.
  • Onyx/champagne grade; generous negative space.

Don't

  • Bright clinical stock or blue-white light.
  • Cheesy smiles, thumbs-up, fake teamwork.
  • Rainbow product shots or busy collages.
